What Is a Mackinaw Jacket?

A mackinaw is a short, double-breasted or single-breasted coat made from thick, felted wool, usually in a bold plaid. It is one of the hardest-wearing jackets ever made.

6 minute read · Reviewed November 2026

I. Where the name comes from

The story begins around 1811 at Fort Mackinac, on the straits between Lake Huron and Lake Michigan. With a hard winter coming and uniforms late, wool blankets kept in the fort stores were cut and sewn into short coats. The name of the place stuck to the jacket.

II. The cut

  • Ends around the hip, so it does not drag in snow or catch on brush.
  • Often double-breasted, with a wide collar that turns up against wind.
  • Large patch or flap pockets, usually four.
  • Roomy through the shoulders for swinging an axe or paddling.

III. The cloth

Mackinaw cloth is heavy wool, often 24 to 30 ounces per yard, that has been fulled or felted so the fibres lock together. This makes it dense, wind-resistant and warm even when damp. It also sheds light rain, because wool fibres naturally resist water on the surface.

IV. Mackinaw or shirt jacket?

A shirt jacket is cut like a shirt from lighter wool, often around 16 ounces, and is meant for layering. A mackinaw is a true outer coat: heavier, shorter and boxier. If you can wear it over a sweater on a freezing morning, it is probably a mackinaw.

V. Wearing one today

A mackinaw pairs well with jeans, work trousers and boots. In milder weather, wear it open over a flannel shirt. In real cold, add a sweater and a wool hat. Choose a size that leaves room for a layer without hanging off the shoulders.
